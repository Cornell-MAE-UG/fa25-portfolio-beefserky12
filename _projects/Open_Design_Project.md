<h2 id="client-pitch">Milestone 1: Client Pitch</h2>

### Problem Statement
[cite_start]Current removal methods for Spotted Lanternflies (SLF)—such as shaking, suction, brushing, or high-force airflow—risk damaging grapes or interfering with harvesting machinery[cite: 2]. [cite_start]There is currently no effective way to remove SLF during harvest without harming the crop[cite: 2].

### Impact & Vision
[cite_start]Because SLF feed on sap, they weaken vines and reduce fruit yield[cite: 2]. [cite_start]Our goal is to eliminate SLF while preserving grapevine health[cite: 2]. [cite_start]The long-term vision is a cylindrical, autonomous unit approximately the height of vineyard trellises that guides and traps insects[cite: 2].

### The Concept
* [cite_start]**Lure:** The device uses scents, sound frequencies, and sugary sap to lure SLF away from vines[cite: 2].
* [cite_start]**Mechanism:** A motion sensor activates a low-pressure vacuum to pull insects inside[cite: 2].
* [cite_start]**Execution:** A rotating blade guides them into an execution chamber where they are eliminated[cite: 2].
* [cite_start]**Advantage:** The system is designed for low maintenance and ease of installation[cite: 2].

### Key Risks
* [cite_start]**Competitive Attraction:** Natural grapevine phloem may be more appealing than artificial lures during ripening[cite: 2].
* [cite_start]**SLF Evasion:** The mechanical rotation might trigger the SLF flight response before they are captured[cite: 2].

<h2 id="functional-prototype">Milestone 2: Functional Prototype</h2>

### Purpose of the Prototype
[cite_start]This prototype validates the mechanical assembly of the "SLF Crusher"[cite: 3]. [cite_start]We specifically tested the ability of the rotating **reaper wall** and **peg plate** to guide pests into the collection chamber[cite: 3].

### Assembly & Components
[cite_start]The system uses a motor-driven D-profile shaft to rotate the internal components[cite: 3].

| Object Description | Material/Fabrication | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reaper Wall** | 3D Printed PLA | [cite_start]Rotating paddle/blade to guide insects [cite: 3] |
| **Peg Plate** | 3D Printed PLA | [cite_start]Stationary plate with 28 pegs for convergence [cite: 3] |
| **Rotating Floor** | 3D Printed PLA | [cite_start]Creates an opening for insects to fall [cite: 3] |
| **Death Chamber** | 3D Printed PLA | [cite_start]Final collection container [cite: 3] |

### Testing Results
[cite_start]We simulated Spotted Lanternflies using crumbled paper balls to measure success across three criteria[cite: 3]:

* [cite_start]**Chamber Drop Test:** Whether paper balls reliably fall into the intended chamber[cite: 3].
    * [cite_start]**Result:** $5/5$ successful drops[cite: 3].
* [cite_start]**Peg Movement Test:** Whether the pegs guide or jam the paper balls[cite: 3].
    * [cite_start]**Result:** The paper balls were crushed by the pegs rather than guided to the center[cite: 3].
* [cite_start]**Manual Spin Test:** Checking for smooth mechanical operation and friction[cite: 3].
    * [cite_start]**Result:** Faced resistance during rotation, causing the entire trap to rotate due to friction[cite: 3].

### Outcome & Next Iteration
[cite_start]To improve the design for the final submission, we will implement the following[cite: 3]:
1. [cite_start]**Increased Curvature:** Redesigning the reaper wall path to guide rather than crush the flies[cite: 3].
2. [cite_start]**Larger Opening:** Increasing the diameter of the drop hole to accommodate multiple flies[cite: 3].
3. [cite_start]**Stability:** Adding weights to the bottom so the container remains stationary during rotation[cite: 3].
4. [cite_start]**Tolerances:** Adding more clearance between interlocking pegs to reduce mechanical resistance[cite: 3].
